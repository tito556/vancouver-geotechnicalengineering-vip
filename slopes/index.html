Slopes & Walls in Vancouver

Knowledgeable. Thorough. Resourceful.

LEARN MORE

Slope stability and retaining wall engineering in Vancouver demand a rigorous understanding of the region's complex glacial geology and high seismic hazard. Our services encompass the full lifecycle of geotechnical assessment, from initial site characterization to long-term performance monitoring of earth retention systems. The Metro Vancouver area is underlain by a challenging sequence of advance glacial till, glaciomarine stony clay, and Capilano sediments, often with artesian groundwater conditions that directly influence lateral earth pressures. All investigations are conducted in accordance with the BC Building Code and Vancouver Building By-law, which reference the seismic provisions of the National Building Code of Canada (NBC 2020) for a site-specific Seismic Hazard Class typically ranging from C to E. A proper investigation begins with defining the stratigraphy through CPT (Cone Penetration Test) soundings, which provide a near-continuous profile of tip resistance and pore pressure, critical for identifying weak clay seams that govern global stability.

Our methodology aligns with the Canadian Foundation Engineering Manual (CFEM) and CSA standards, employing limit equilibrium analysis for static conditions and deformation-based models for the seismic design of mechanically stabilized earth (MSE) walls and soil nail systems. We quantify soil parameters through specialized laboratory testing, including consolidated-undrained triaxial tests with pore pressure measurement, which are essential for determining effective stress strength envelopes in the over-consolidated local tills. The characterization of fine-grained units relies on precise Atterberg limits to evaluate plasticity and potential for creep, while grain size analysis (sieve + hydrometer) confirms the gradation of granular backfill and drainage layers. For fill compaction control behind walls, we perform the field density test (sand cone method), a standard referenced in ASTM D1556, to verify that structural backfill meets the project-specified relative compaction, typically 95% to 100% of Standard Proctor maximum dry density.

Typical Vancouver projects range from deep permanent shoring for underground parkades in the dense downtown core to reinforced soil slopes stabilizing residential properties along the North Shore's steep terrain. We routinely design cantilever and anchored soldier pile walls to retain cuts exceeding 10 meters in the glacial till, where temporary excavation support requires analysis of short-term undrained loading. For major infrastructure along the Fraser River corridor, we address lateral spreading potential in loose alluvial deposits using In-Situ data, particularly shear wave velocity profiles from seismic CPT, to estimate liquefaction-induced deformations and their impact on adjacent retaining structures. Each design must satisfy the strict deformation tolerances set by the City of Vancouver to protect adjacent sensitive heritage structures and utilities.

The process concludes with a geotechnical design report delivering a comprehensive factual and interpretative package, including pore water pressure distribution models, seismic earth pressure calculations per the NBC, and global and internal stability outputs. We provide a clear value proposition by integrating advanced constitutive modeling with practical construction experience, ensuring that the recommended slope geometry or wall section is both robust and constructible given Vancouver's challenging wet weather and restricted access conditions. The final deliverable includes detailed instrumented monitoring specifications, such as inclinometers and piezometers, to validate design assumptions during and after construction, ensuring the long-term resilience of the asset against both static creep and the region's significant seismic demand.

Quick answers

What is the difference between an active and a passive anchor for a Vancouver excavation?

An active anchor is stressed immediately after grout curing to apply a pre-compressive force to the wall, limiting initial deflection—critical when shoring next to a heritage building in Gastown. A passive anchor, often a fully grouted bar, is not stressed until the soil mass begins to move; it is better suited for temporary cuts where minor deformation is acceptable. In Vancouver's stiff till, active strand anchors allow for tighter deflection tolerances under the NBCC serviceability limits.

How much does anchor design and testing cost for a typical Vancouver site?

For a standard program including design review and two sacrificial proof tests, budgets typically range from CA$1,470 to CA$5,440, depending on whether you need a simple bar anchor verification in till or a comprehensive strand anchor program with extended creep monitoring in the marine clays. Mobilization costs within the Metro Vancouver area are factored into this range, but difficult access sites on the North Shore may require additional logistics.

Can you test anchors in the rain during a Vancouver winter?

Yes, the hydraulic jack and load cell systems are fully sealed for operation in heavy precipitation, which is a standard requirement for fieldwork from November through March in the Lower Mainland. The main concern during winter testing is not the rain itself but the temperature effect on the hydraulic oil viscosity; we allow a warm-up cycle for the power pack and record the ambient temperature to correct the pressure transducer readings if needed.
