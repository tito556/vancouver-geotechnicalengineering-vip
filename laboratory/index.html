Laboratory in Vancouver

Knowledgeable. Thorough. Resourceful.

Geotechnical laboratory testing in Vancouver forms a critical component of subsurface characterization, bridging field observations with the precise physical and mechanical properties required for safe foundation design. The region’s complex geology—dominated by glacial till, marine silts, sensitive clays, and liquefiable sands from the Fraser River delta—demands rigorous testing protocols to mitigate risks associated with slope instability, seismic activity, and consolidation settlement. A comprehensive laboratory program typically supports our geotechnical investigation findings and refines soil parameters initially estimated through cone penetration testing (CPT), ensuring compliance with the BC Building Code and standards set by Engineers and Geoscientists BC.

Our Vancouver laboratory operates in strict accordance with Canadian Standards Association (CSA) and ASTM International methods adapted for local conditions. Classification testing follows CSA A23.2/ ASTM D422 for grain size analysis, combining mechanical sieving for coarse fractions with hydrometer analysis for fines, which is essential for identifying the silt and clay matrices prevalent in False Creek and Richmond deltaic deposits. The determination of Atterberg limits per CSA A23.2/ASTM D4318 quantifies liquid and plastic limits, directly correlating to the sensitivity and volume change potential of local glaciolacustrine clays. For direct density verification, we perform field density tests using the sand cone method, with laboratory maximum dry density from standard Proctor tests (ASTM D698) providing the reference baseline for compaction control on structural fill and dike construction projects.

Typical Vancouver projects requiring advanced laboratory analysis include deep excavations for parkade construction in the downtown core, where triaxial shear and oedometer consolidation tests define the stress-strain behavior of overconsolidated till. High-rise foundations in the Marine Gateway area often rely on laboratory permeability tests and unconfined compressive strength tests on siltstone bedrock to calibrate shoring and dewatering designs. Transportation infrastructure, such as the Broadway Subway extension, utilizes resilient modulus and California Bearing Ratio (CBR) tests on subgrade soils to validate pavement structure designs under saturated winter conditions. These programs are frequently integrated with In-Situ correlations to maximize data reliability across heterogenous geological units.

The testing process begins with the careful preservation and transport of Shelby tube or block samples from our drilling crews to the laboratory, where chain-of-custody documentation ensures traceability. Clients receive a detailed geotechnical data report containing individual test results, graphical summaries, and interpretation of engineering parameters such as effective friction angle, preconsolidation pressure, and hydraulic conductivity. This deliverable provides the essential input for geotechnical engineers to perform bearing capacity calculations, settlement analyses, and seismic liquefaction assessments, ultimately de-risking the foundation design and construction phases in Vancouver's challenging urban and geological environment.

Quick answers

What is the difference between an active and a passive anchor for a Vancouver excavation?

An active anchor is stressed immediately after grout curing to apply a pre-compressive force to the wall, limiting initial deflection—critical when shoring next to a heritage building in Gastown. A passive anchor, often a fully grouted bar, is not stressed until the soil mass begins to move; it is better suited for temporary cuts where minor deformation is acceptable. In Vancouver's stiff till, active strand anchors allow for tighter deflection tolerances under the NBCC serviceability limits.

How much does anchor design and testing cost for a typical Vancouver site?

For a standard program including design review and two sacrificial proof tests, budgets typically range from CA$1,470 to CA$5,440, depending on whether you need a simple bar anchor verification in till or a comprehensive strand anchor program with extended creep monitoring in the marine clays. Mobilization costs within the Metro Vancouver area are factored into this range, but difficult access sites on the North Shore may require additional logistics.

Can you test anchors in the rain during a Vancouver winter?

Yes, the hydraulic jack and load cell systems are fully sealed for operation in heavy precipitation, which is a standard requirement for fieldwork from November through March in the Lower Mainland. The main concern during winter testing is not the rain itself but the temperature effect on the hydraulic oil viscosity; we allow a warm-up cycle for the power pack and record the ambient temperature to correct the pressure transducer readings if needed.
