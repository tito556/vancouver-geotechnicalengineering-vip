Underground Excavations in Vancouver

Knowledgeable. Thorough. Resourceful.

LEARN MORE

Underground excavations in Vancouver require a specialized understanding of the region’s complex glacial geology, which includes lodgement till, glaciomarine silts, and the challenging presence of the Quadra Sand formation. Our geotechnical services cover the full lifecycle of subsurface projects, from preliminary investigation and rock mass characterization to construction support for tunnels, shafts, and deep basements. The work is governed by the BC Building Code and local municipal bylaws, with all design and execution adhering to the strict safety requirements of WorkSafeBC for confined spaces and temporary works. A robust laboratory testing program on recovered core and disturbed samples is essential for determining the engineering properties of the often overconsolidated soils and weak bedrock that dominate the Metro Vancouver basin.

A rigorous site characterization methodology is the foundation of any successful underground project in this seismically active zone. We integrate targeted Cone Penetration Test (CPT) soundings to delineate stratigraphy and detect loose, potentially liquefiable layers, with dedicated in-situ testing programs that include pressuremeter tests for direct measurement of soil stiffness and in-situ stress. All field and laboratory procedures strictly follow CSA (Canadian Standards Association) and ASTM International standards. This includes index property tests such as grain size analysis by combined sieve and hydrometer to classify fine-grained glacial deposits, and Atterberg limits determination to assess the plasticity and shrink-swell potential of silts and clays, which are critical parameters for predicting stand-up time and ground behavior during excavation.

Typical projects in Vancouver’s dense urban environment present unique geotechnical challenges. Deep excavations for parkade foundations in the downtown core must manage significant groundwater drawdown and lateral earth pressures, while tunnel boring for the Broadway Subway or regional sewer systems requires precise forecasting of abrasivity and face stability through mixed-ground conditions. On sloping terrain, such as in West Vancouver or Burnaby Mountain, excavation stability is influenced by colluvial deposits and weathered rock interfaces. For utility trenching and shaft installations, we frequently deploy the field density test (sand cone method) to verify engineered backfill compaction, ensuring compliance with the City of Vancouver’s strict restoration standards and mitigating future settlement within the right-of-way.

Our process delivers a clear and actionable design path from initial exploration to construction completion. We synthesize all gathered data into interpretative reports that include geotechnical parameters, ground movement predictions, and a detailed excavation support design tailored to Vancouver’s regulatory landscape. The key deliverable is a de-risked project, where potential hazards like boulder fields, flowing sands, or artesian pressures are identified and mitigated before a shovel breaks ground. This integrated approach, combining local geological insight with advanced testing, provides developers, contractors, and municipal agencies the confidence that their underground work will proceed safely, on schedule, and with a minimized impact on the surrounding urban fabric.

Quick answers

What is the difference between an active and a passive anchor for a Vancouver excavation?

An active anchor is stressed immediately after grout curing to apply a pre-compressive force to the wall, limiting initial deflection—critical when shoring next to a heritage building in Gastown. A passive anchor, often a fully grouted bar, is not stressed until the soil mass begins to move; it is better suited for temporary cuts where minor deformation is acceptable. In Vancouver's stiff till, active strand anchors allow for tighter deflection tolerances under the NBCC serviceability limits.

How much does anchor design and testing cost for a typical Vancouver site?

For a standard program including design review and two sacrificial proof tests, budgets typically range from CA$1,470 to CA$5,440, depending on whether you need a simple bar anchor verification in till or a comprehensive strand anchor program with extended creep monitoring in the marine clays. Mobilization costs within the Metro Vancouver area are factored into this range, but difficult access sites on the North Shore may require additional logistics.

Can you test anchors in the rain during a Vancouver winter?

Yes, the hydraulic jack and load cell systems are fully sealed for operation in heavy precipitation, which is a standard requirement for fieldwork from November through March in the Lower Mainland. The main concern during winter testing is not the rain itself but the temperature effect on the hydraulic oil viscosity; we allow a warm-up cycle for the power pack and record the ambient temperature to correct the pressure transducer readings if needed.
