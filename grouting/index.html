Vancouver sits on a complex foundation of glacial till, marine clays, and deltaic sands—layers that shift dramatically within a single city block. At 49.2609°N, the city’s geology is a direct legacy of the Cordilleran ice sheet, and it demands grouting designs that are anything but generic. We develop injection programs that stabilize these erratic deposits, control groundwater during deep excavations, and fill voids in buried creek beds like those under False Creek. A standard mix won’t work here. Our team ties every grouting specification to site-specific data, ensuring that the design matches the real conditions uncovered during the investigation phase.

A grouting program without a refusal criterion is just pumping. We define when to stop based on pressure, volume, and ground heave.

Process overview

The most common mistake we see in Vancouver is treating compaction grouting as a one-size-fits-all fix for loose fills. Contractors pump grout at high pressure without mapping the underground utilities or the sensitive clay lenses, and they end up heaving the adjacent sidewalk or fracturing a water main. Our approach is different. We define refusal criteria based on volume limits and pressure thresholds, not just volume. For permeation grouting in the sandy soils north of Marine Drive, we specify microfine cements that penetrate without clogging the formation. In the dense till of the North Shore, we often switch to jet grouting to create soilcrete columns where permeation is impossible. The design always includes a real-time monitoring plan to track ground movement and adjust injection parameters on the fly.

Local context

In Vancouver’s West End, we’ve seen how an unvented compaction grout program can blow out a century-old brick sewer line that no as-built drawing captured. The risk isn’t theoretical. Glaciomarine silts can liquefy under cyclic loading, and untreated, they lose strength during a crustal or subduction earthquake. A grouting design that ignores seismic pore pressure buildup leaves the foundation vulnerable to settlement and lateral spread. We mitigate this by specifying grout hole geometry that reinforces the critical failure wedge identified in the slope stability or liquefaction analysis. Every design also includes a contingency for karst-like features in the limestone bedrock pockets east of Boundary Road—voids that can swallow grout volume without warning.

Quick answers

What grouting method works best in Vancouver’s glacial till?

Glacial till is dense and low-permeability, so permeation grouting usually fails. We specify jet grouting to erode and mix the till with cement slurry, forming soilcrete columns. For compaction grouting in the overlying fills, we use low-slump mortar at 2–4 MPa to densify without fracturing the till interface.

How do you design grouting for seismic liquefaction mitigation?

We map the liquefiable layer from CPT data, then design a grid of compaction grout points that densify the sand to a target SPT N-value. The spacing and injection sequence follow a staged pattern to avoid pore pressure buildup. Post-grouting, we verify with new CPT soundings that the soil meets the cyclic resistance ratio required by NBCC 2020.

What does a grouting design package include?

Every package includes a plan view with injection point coordinates, cross-sections showing treatment depth and target soil units, refusal criteria (pressure, volume, heave limits), grout mix design, QA/QC testing schedule, and a contingency plan for unexpected ground conditions. We also include a monitoring layout with survey points and inclinometer locations if near existing structures.

How much does a grouting design cost in Vancouver?

The design fee ranges from CA$1,830 to CA$5,910, depending on the project scale and the number of grouting methods involved. A simple compaction grouting layout for a single house foundation is at the lower end. A full jet grouting design for a deep excavation with adjacent heritage buildings falls at the higher end.

How long does the design process take?

For a typical residential or small commercial project, we deliver the initial design within 10 business days after receiving the geotechnical investigation report. Complex projects with multiple grouting techniques and staged construction may take 3–4 weeks, including review meetings and revisions.
