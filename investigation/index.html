Investigation in Vancouver

Knowledgeable. Thorough. Resourceful.

LEARN MORE

Geotechnical investigation in Vancouver is a critical first step for any construction project, designed to characterize subsurface conditions and mitigate risk in a region shaped by complex glacial and post-glacial geology. Our site-specific programs address the unique challenges of the Lower Mainland, including glacially over-consolidated till, liquefiable deltaic sands, and sensitive marine silts. These investigations are guided by the foundations of local regulatory knowledge, ensuring compliance with the City of Vancouver’s Building Bylaw and the seismic hazard requirements of the current National Building Code of Canada. A thorough investigation translates these geological constraints into reliable geotechnical parameters for safe and economical design.

Our field methodology integrates a range of intrusive techniques executed in strict accordance with the Canadian Foundation Engineering Manual and relevant ASTM standards. We deploy a combination of continuous sampling with hollow-stem augers and advanced In-Situ to capture a complete geotechnical profile. A primary tool is the CPT (Cone Penetration Test), which provides near-continuous data on soil stratigraphy and is essential for liquefaction assessment in Vancouver’s high-seismicity setting. For cohesive soils, we complement this with In-Situ such as field vane shear tests to measure undrained strength in sensitive clays. The density of granular structural fills is verified through nuclear gauge testing or the sand cone method, ensuring compaction meets project specifications.

These investigative techniques support a wide spectrum of projects across Vancouver’s diverse landscape. Low-to-mid-rise residential developments in areas like Kitsilano often require careful logging of fill and soft soils overlying dense till. For high-rise towers in the downtown core or the Broadway Plan corridor, deep investigations with CPT are mandatory for seismic design and deep excavation support. Infrastructure projects, including the Broadway Subway extension, rely on this data for tunnel alignment design. Across all project types, the collected samples are transferred to our laboratory for a suite of index and performance tests to validate field classifications and derive engineering properties.

The investigative process delivers a clear progression from field data to actionable engineering recommendations. After completing the drilling and testing program, we subject all samples to detailed laboratory analysis, including grain size analysis via sieves and hydrometer to classify the soil, and Atterberg limits testing to define the plasticity of fine-grained strata. The final deliverable is a comprehensive geotechnical report containing site plans, borehole logs, laboratory results, and thorough analysis. This document provides clear recommendations for foundation design, seismic site class, and excavation safety, giving our clients the engineered certainty required to proceed with confidence.

Quick answers

What is the difference between an active and a passive anchor for a Vancouver excavation?

An active anchor is stressed immediately after grout curing to apply a pre-compressive force to the wall, limiting initial deflection—critical when shoring next to a heritage building in Gastown. A passive anchor, often a fully grouted bar, is not stressed until the soil mass begins to move; it is better suited for temporary cuts where minor deformation is acceptable. In Vancouver's stiff till, active strand anchors allow for tighter deflection tolerances under the NBCC serviceability limits.

How much does anchor design and testing cost for a typical Vancouver site?

For a standard program including design review and two sacrificial proof tests, budgets typically range from CA$1,470 to CA$5,440, depending on whether you need a simple bar anchor verification in till or a comprehensive strand anchor program with extended creep monitoring in the marine clays. Mobilization costs within the Metro Vancouver area are factored into this range, but difficult access sites on the North Shore may require additional logistics.

Can you test anchors in the rain during a Vancouver winter?

Yes, the hydraulic jack and load cell systems are fully sealed for operation in heavy precipitation, which is a standard requirement for fieldwork from November through March in the Lower Mainland. The main concern during winter testing is not the rain itself but the temperature effect on the hydraulic oil viscosity; we allow a warm-up cycle for the power pack and record the ambient temperature to correct the pressure transducer readings if needed.
